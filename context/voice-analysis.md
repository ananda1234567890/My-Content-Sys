## Writing Personality

Ananda is a practitioner first. He doesn't write like a teacher — he writes like someone who just discovered something cool and can't wait to share it. His curiosity is his defining trait: when he learns something, he has 100 questions and he takes the audience on that same journey.

## Tone Markers

- **Direct and fast** — gets to the point immediately, no warm-up preamble
- **Enthusiastic about craft** — genuine excitement about editing tricks and new tools, not performed enthusiasm
- **Curious framing** — often poses questions, explores "what if" scenarios, breaks things down to first principles
- **Specific and technical** — uses real numbers, real tool names, real techniques — not vague generalities
- **Humble confidence** — knows his craft deeply but doesn't showboat; lets the knowledge speak

## What to Avoid

- Motivational/hustle content ("wake up at 5am", "your mindset is the problem")
- Personal story as the main hook (₹3000 → $4000 narrative is background, not foreground)
- Generic creator advice that anyone could write
- Corporate or agency-speak
- Overclaiming ("I cracked the algorithm")

## Hook Patterns (Inferred)

Since content focus is craft/industry, hooks should lead with:
- A surprising fact about YouTube/video editing ("Most editors don't know that...")
- A specific technique reveal ("This one cut pattern tripled retention for a client")
- A reaction to industry news ("YouTube just changed X. Here's what it means for editors")
- A contrarian take ("Everyone's using [tool] wrong")
- A "just learned this" moment ("Spent 3 hours figuring this out so you don't have to")

## Post Structure (Target)

- **Hook:** 1-2 lines max, pattern-interrupt or specific insight
- **Body:** Practical breakdown — numbered lists, short paragraphs, real examples
- **Close:** Observation or open question — not a forced CTA
- **Length:** Medium (300-600 words on LinkedIn) — enough to be substantive, not a wall of text
