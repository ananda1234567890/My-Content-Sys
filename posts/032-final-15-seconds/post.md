# Your Final 15 Seconds Are as Important as Your Hook

**Date created:** 2026-06-13
**Source:** @editingexplained
**Format:** AI Infographic
**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Status:** Draft

## Post Text (copy-paste ready)

Your hook gets them in
Your last 15 seconds decide if they remember you

Wait. Read that again.

Most editors obsess over the opening hook. And yeah, the first few seconds matter. But the final 15 seconds? Almost nobody thinks about that part strategically.

→ Viewers who make it to the end of your video are the most likely to follow, comment, and share
→ A weak ending is like a great conversation that just stops mid-sentence
→ Most videos end with a CTA or a fade. That's not an ending. That's a stop.

Here's the thing. The last 15 seconds should work like a bridge, not a door.

Do this:
→ Ego-bait close. End with a statement that makes the viewer feel status for having watched. "The editors getting consistent clients in 2025 already know this." That's not a CTA. That's a mirror.
→ Perceived value close. Spend the last 15 seconds demonstrating something genuinely impressive: a concept, a workflow, a level of detail that makes viewers feel they just learned something high-level by staying till the end.
→ Authority close. Flash a brief look at something advanced without explaining it. A complex timeline, a masterclass setup, an advanced workflow step. The curiosity does the work.

That's it. Three ways to close a video so viewers walk away with something, not just away.

The open gets the view
The CLOSE gets the follower

P.S. Pick one of these for your next video and actually try it. You'll notice the difference in comments within days :)
