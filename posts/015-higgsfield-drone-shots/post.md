## Post Text (copy-paste ready)

Higgsfield just made cinematic drone b-roll accessible to solo creators and I feel like nobody is ACTUALLY talking about it.

Drone operators charge anywhere from $300 to $1,000+ per shoot.
Here's how to get the same shot from a static photo.

That will actually hold up in real content.

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟭: Capture Your Source Shot
Take a static photo or short clip in any outdoor location. Environmental context is what makes the motion look real.
→ Any smartphone photo works
→ Natural light helps but is not required
→ Shoot wider than you think. AI needs surrounding environment to move the camera convincingly.

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟮: Upload to Higgsfield
Go to the Higgsfield platform and upload your asset.
→ Select the Kling 2.6 model specifically, not the default
→ Kling 2.6 handles camera movement more fluidly than earlier versions

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟯: Write a Precise Movement Prompt
Most people get this wrong. Describe the camera movement, not the vibe.
1) Weak: "make this cinematic"
2) Better: "drone shot slowly pulling upward and back, revealing the surroundings"
3) Best: "smooth aerial pullback from eye level, rising steadily, subject stays centered as the environment expands into frame"

Now you have drone b-roll from a photo you took on your phone.

The barrier to high-production establishing shots just dropped.
If budget was the reason you weren't using aerial b-roll, that excuse is gone.

Worth it?
