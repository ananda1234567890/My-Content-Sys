# 5 Fonts and What Each One Signals to Your Viewer

**Date created:** 2026-06-13
**Source:** @hey.deshna
**Format:** Carousel
**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Status:** Draft

## Post Text (copy-paste ready)

Your font is talking to your viewer before a single word of your script plays.

Most editors pick fonts based on what looks cool. The best editors pick fonts based on what the content needs to feel like.

After studying the visual choices of hundreds of high-performing creators
And looking at which typography decisions correlated with strong audience trust and retention
I've found five fonts that cover almost every content tone you'd need
Each one sends a very specific signal. Here's the breakdown.

Montserrat
The clean, modern workhorse. Signals trustworthiness and professionalism without feeling corporate. Works for educational content, talking-head tutorials, and explainer-style videos. Viewers process it as credible before they consciously register the choice.

Oswald
Bold, condensed, authoritative. Use this when the content needs to hit hard. Business breakdowns, direct-response content, anything where authority matters more than warmth. Feels like someone who means business.

Playfair Display
Elegant, high-contrast, premium. If your content is luxury-adjacent, design-led, or targets a high-income audience, this is the font. It signals quality before the viewer reads a single word.

Bebas Neue
Loud, cinematic, built for impact. Sports, action, gaming, hype-style editing. This is the font for when you need the text itself to feel like part of the energy of the video.

DM Sans
Minimal, tech-forward, editorial. Works for productivity content, creator economy posts, anything that needs to feel modern but not aggressive. Readers trust DM Sans the same way they trust a clean, well-designed app.

Typography isn't decoration
Every font choice is a signal your viewer processes before they decide whether to keep watching.
