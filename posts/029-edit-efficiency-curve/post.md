## Post Text (copy-paste ready)

Most editors don't have a cutting problem
They have a stopping problem

Yup That's actually what's happening

→ You finish a sequence. Looks solid
→ You watch it back. Tweak two things
→ You watch it again. Tweak three more
→ An hour later you're basically back where you started

This loop has a name. It's called the Efficiency Curve. And most editors are stuck inside it without realizing.

Here's the thing.

The best editors I've watched work don't actually edit faster. They make fewer decisions during the edit, because they already made those decisions before they opened the software.

So how do you break the loop?

Here's how to get more done from ONE focused editing session.

Do this:
→ Plan before you touch the software. Even 30 minutes with a rough storyboard means every cut is basically decided before you open Premiere. You're assembling, not figuring it out on the fly
→ Set a stopping rule. At some point, another pass doesn't make the video better, it just makes it different. Knowing where that line is, and actually stopping there, is a real skill
→ Go deeper on keyboard shortcuts. Not just for cutting. Panel switching, applying effects, setting export presets. The mouse is the slowest tool in an edit suite, tbh

The editor who plans for 30 minutes and edits for 2 hours consistently beats the one who just opens Premiere and edits for 4.

P.S. The re-edit spiral isn't perfectionism. It's just what happens when you don't decide in advance when you're done :)
