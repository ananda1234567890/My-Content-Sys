## Post Text (copy-paste ready)

Video editors pay for third-party motion tracking plugins all the time.

Planar tracking has been inside After Effects this whole time.

That will actually save you hours:

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟭: Open the Tracker panel
Go to Window in After Effects and open the Tracker panel. Look for "Perspective Corner Pin" in the track type options. Select it before you do anything else. That setting is what tells the tracker to follow a flat surface across all its axes.

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟮: Draw your mask and analyze
Draw a mask around the surface you want to track — a phone screen, a wall, a whiteboard, a hand. Hit "Analyze Forward" and let After Effects do the frame-by-frame analysis.
→ The tracker reads the surface's perspective changes and movement across every frame
→ Takes a few seconds on short clips, longer on heavier footage

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟯: Link the track to your layer
Once the analysis is done, click "Edit Target" and select the layer you want to pin to the tracked surface — your text, a graphic, a logo, anything.
→ Apply the tracking data to that layer
→ The layer now follows the surface's exact movement through the whole clip

Now your motion graphic moves like it was shot in camera.

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟰: Position and trim
Place the text or element exactly where you want it on the surface. Trim the clip and check the tracking at a few key points to confirm it's holding.

That's it. No third-party plugin. No manual keyframing. No frame-by-frame adjustments.

You can go from raw footage to tracked motion graphics in under five minutes once you've done it a couple times.

It's been sitting there the whole time.
