# Storyboard Before You Edit

**Date created:** 2026-06-13
**Source:** @josephswork
**Format:** Carousel
**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Status:** Draft

## Post Text (copy-paste ready)

Editors who plan before they open their NLE finish faster than editors who just start cutting.

Not slightly faster. Like, embarrassingly faster.

Here's the workflow:

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟭: Storyboard Before You Touch Premiere
Open Figma (or a plain text doc) before you load any footage
→ Map out the rough sequence of scenes
→ Note where the cuts happen and why
→ Decide the pacing before the timeline opens

Spend 1 to 2 hours here. Sounds slow. Saves 3.

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟮: Do the Technical Edit in One Pass
Once the storyboard is locked, open Premiere Pro and execute it
→ Cut and sequence the clips
→ Level the audio
→ Set the rough color pass

This phase is fast now because every decision was already made in Step 1. You're assembling, not figuring it out on the timeline.

𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝟯: Add the Polish Layer Last
This is where subtitles, text animations, sound design, and motion graphics go
→ Captions and supers
→ Transitions and animation elements
→ Sound effects and music balancing

That's it. Three phases, completely separated.

Most editors try to do all three at once. That's why the timeline gets messy and the edit takes twice as long as it should.

Structure the process and the edit almost edits itself.

Worth trying once.
