## Post Text (copy-paste ready)

Your text is getting lost in the background and it has nothing to do with the font.

Wait, what?

Yup. Every editor's first instinct is to mess with the font — try a bolder weight, increase the size, switch to something cleaner. But the real problem is contrast, and there's a one-step fix inside Premiere Pro that most editors skip entirely.

A semi-transparent solid color layer under the text.

Do this:
→ Add a new solid color layer (black works for almost everything) below your text layer in the sequence
→ Set the opacity somewhere between 40% and 70% depending on how bright your footage is
→ Size the layer to just cover the text area, or let it vignette the full frame

That's it. The layer creates a separation between the text and whatever is happening in the video. The viewer's eye locks onto the text automatically.

Works on busy outdoor shots, motion blur, fast-cutting sequences, anything.

From there, adjust the opacity until it feels subtle. The goal is invisible support, not a black box.

One layer. No plugins. Applies to every project you'll ever work on.

P.S. Same technique works in After Effects, DaVinci, CapCut, any editor with opacity controls :)
