## Post Text (copy-paste ready)

Most video editors try to force one workflow onto every project.

That's what's costing them hours they don't know they're losing.

Many believe a consistent editing process means consistent output...

The truth?

Different video styles need different starting points entirely. Force the same process onto every format and you're fighting the footage instead of editing it.

After years cutting cinematic pieces, vlogs, and viral talking heads.
And editing across all three for clients with completely different goals.
I've watched editors waste real time because they picked the wrong first step.
(took me way too long to figure this out tbh)
I've mapped out the three starting patterns that actually work.

Cinematic
1. Lock in the story first
2. Choose the music next
3. Edit to the story and music together
The emotion and pacing are determined by the narrative and the track. Start cutting before you know the story and you'll be re-cutting everything.

Viral Talking Head
1. Start with the raw content. Cut bad takes, dead air, and filler first.
2. Do the primary cuts and tighten the pacing
3. Add subtitles
4. Layer in graphics and animations last
Motion graphics sit on top of a clean, tight edit. Start with them and you'll lose the structure under all the layers.

Vlog
1. Write the full storyline and objective first
2. Select footage that serves the story
3. Build the structure around the narrative arc
A vlog lives or dies by the story. Go in without a clear arc and you'll end up with beautiful B-roll that leads nowhere.

Start at the right point for the right video type and you'll cut real time off every edit.

And you'll wonder how you ever forced one process onto all of them.
